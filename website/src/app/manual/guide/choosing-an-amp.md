The Amp block is the heart of almost every preset. Cathode has 88 amp models, each built from the real amp's schematic stage by stage: the preamp tubes, the tone stack, the phase inverter, the power tubes, the output transformer, the speaker load and the power supply. Because the circuit is modelled rather than a set of measured curves, the knobs interact as they do on the real amp, and the expert settings change real components.

This chapter helps you choose a model, dial its front panel, and understand the expert settings behind it. For every control, knob by knob, see the [Amp block reference](/manual/blocks/circuitamp).

## The amp families

Amps fall into families that share a circuit idea and a sound. Within a family, models differ in gain, headroom and voicing, so try two or three before you settle. Names in **bold** are Cathode's Type names.

### Fender tweed (1950s)

**Tweed Deluxe 5E3**, **Bassman 5F6-A**, **Tweed Twin 5F8-A**, **Champ 5F1**, **Princeton 5F2-A**, and the **Jumped** versions of the Deluxe, Bassman and Twin.

Simple circuits with little negative feedback (or none) and a tone stack that loses little signal. They break up early, compress, and get hairy and loose when cranked. Great for blues, roots rock and raw, woolly crunch. The **Jumped** models link both input channels, as players do with a short cable, for a fuller sound with both volume controls.

### Fender brownface and blackface (1960s)

Brownface: **Concert 6G12**, **Super 6G4**, **Deluxe 6G3**, **Vibrolux 6G11**.
Blackface and its relatives: **Blackface** (a generic Deluxe and Twin-style model), **Deluxe Reverb**, **Twin Reverb**, **Super Reverb**, **Princeton Reverb**, **Princeton AA964**, **Vibroverb AB763**, **Vibrolux Reverb AA964**, **Bandmaster AB763**, **Tremolux AA763**.

The classic clean platform: scooped mids, sparkling top, and plenty of headroom in the bigger models. The Twin and Bandmaster stay clean loud; the Deluxe Reverb and Princeton break up sooner and sweeter. Many have their own vibrato (the switch to the **Vibrato Ch**, then **Speed** and **Intensity**). Use them for clean, country, funk, jazz, surf, and as a pedal platform.

### Later Fenders

**Hot Rod Deluxe**, **Blues Junior**, **Vibro-King**.

More gain and a master volume. The Hot Rod Deluxe has a clean channel (**Normal Ch**) and a drive channel with **More Drive**.

### Marshall: the early amps

**JTM45**, **Bluesbreaker 1962**, **Plexi** (a generic 50 W Super Lead), **Plexi 100W**, **1959SLP**, **1987X**, **1959T Tremolo**, **Major 1967**, **18 W 1974X**.

The sound of 1960s and 1970s rock. No master volume: the volume controls drive the whole amp, so these models crunch and sing when turned up. Most have two channels (normal and treble or bright), with a **Jumper** switch to link them, and a second volume (**Normal Vol**, **Loudness 2** or **Volume II**) to blend the darker channel in. The JTM45 and Bluesbreaker are rounder and looser; the Plexi family tighter and brighter; the Major is huge and clean until pushed very hard.

### Marshall: master volume and later

**JCM800 2203**, **JCM800 2204**, **Silver Jubilee**, **JCM900 4100**, **JCM900 4502**, **DSL100**, **JVM410H OD**, **JVM410H Clean/Crunch**, **JMP-1**, **AFD100**, and the solid-state **Valvestate 8080**.

A master volume and more preamp gain: hard rock, classic metal, punk. The JCM800 is the reference crunch-to-lead amp of the 1980s; it loves a boost in front. The Silver Jubilee has a smoother, more compressed lead (**Boost**, **Rhythm Clip**). The AFD100 is a hot-rodded 1980s Marshall voice (its **AFD** switch picks the mode). The JMP-1 is a preamp into a 100 W power amp.

### Other British amps

**Hiwatt DR103**, **Hiwatt DR504**, **Laney GH100L**, **Cornford MK50H II**, **Tiny Terror**.

Hiwatts are loud, clean and firm, with huge headroom and a tight low end: big chords that only break up when hit hard. The Laney GH100L sits between a Marshall and a modern high-gain amp. The Tiny Terror is a small, simple, mid-forward crunch amp.

### Vox and class A

**AC30**, **AC15**, **DC-30**, **Chieftain**, **Hot Cat 30**, **Express**, **Liverpool**.

Chime and jangle: a bright, complex top, little or no negative feedback, and power sections that break up sweetly. These amps have a **Cut** control instead of a presence: it works the other way round, so more Cut means less treble. The AC30's **Normal Ch** switch picks its plainer channel. The DC-30, Chieftain and Hot Cat are boutique amps in this spirit; the Express and Liverpool are the Trainwreck circuits.

### Dumble-style

**ODS 97**, **ODS 70s**.

Big, clean, fast amps with a separate overdrive section (**Overdrive** switch, then the overdrive's own drive and level). Smooth, vocal, touch-sensitive leads that clean up with your picking. Blues, fusion and singer-songwriter tones.

### Hot-rodded and modern British-style

**SLO-100**, **BE-100**, **BE-100 HBE**, **Quick Rod**.

Marshall-like voices with more gain stages. The SLO-100 is the smooth 1990s lead and rhythm amp; the BE-100 a tight, modern hot-rodded Plexi (**Sat**, **Fat**, **C45**, **Voice**); the HBE version has more gain; the Quick Rod has three gears of drive.

### Mesa/Boogie

Mark series: **Mark IIC+**, **Mark IV**, **Mark V**. Rectifiers: **Dual Rectifier**, **Dual Rectifier 3-Ch**, **Triple Rectifier**, **Rect-o-Verb**. And **Lone Star**, **Subway Blues**, and the **TriAxis Rhythm**, **TriAxis Lead 1** and **TriAxis Lead 2** preamp modes.

Two very different families:

- **Mark amps** cascade gain stages for a tight, focused lead and thrash rhythm, and have a five-band graphic EQ (80, 240, 750, 2200 and 6600 Hz) after the preamp. A "V" shape on it (bass and treble bands up, the 750 Hz band down) is the classic setting; go easy, it scoops quickly. The lead drive is **Lead Drive** with its own **Lead Master**.
- **Rectifiers** are loose, thick and saturated, with a huge low end. **Modern** takes the feedback out for a more aggressive, open top; the **Tube Rectifier** and **Spongy** switches soften the supply for a saggier feel.

The Lone Star is a clean and edge-of-breakup amp with a drive channel.

### High gain

**5150**, **5150III**, **Legacy VL100**, **Powerball**, **Savage 120**, **Prometheus**, **Prometheus Clean**.

Modern metal and hard rock. The 5150 and 5150III are tight and aggressive, with a **Resonance** control for the low end; the 5150's **Pre Gain** is the gain and **Post Gain** the master. The Powerball and Savage 120 are German high-gain amps with mid controls of their own (**Focused**, **Contour**). The Legacy VL100 is a smooth lead amp. The Prometheus models are a Lichtlaerm Audio design whose circuit was inferred without a published schematic.

### Solid state

**JC-120** and **Valvestate 8080**.

The JC-120 is a glassy, very clean transistor amp: no breakup, so effects stay pristine. Classic for chorus, new wave, jazz and ambient. The Valvestate 8080 has a tube preamp stage into a solid-state power amp. Tube-only settings (bias, tubes, transformer, speaker load) are not shown on solid-state models.

### Bass and others

**SVT**, **Portaflex B-15N**, **Bassman 135**, **Bass 400**, and the small Gibson **Scout GA-17RVT**.

Bass amps work on guitar too: the SVT and Bass 400 make thick, heavy crunch; the Portaflex is warm and round.

## The front panel

Each model shows its own front panel: the knobs and switches the real amp has, under the real amp's names. A Plexi has **Treble Vol** and **Normal Vol** and no master; a Mark IIC+ has **Volume**, **Lead Drive**, **Lead Master** and the EQ bands; a 5150 has **Pre Gain** and **Post Gain**. The underlying controls are the same for every model (the generic **Gain**, **Bass**, **Middle**, **Treble**, **Presence**, **Depth**, **Cut**, **Master** and the model's switches), which is why a modifier or a scene keeps working when you change the model.

Two views:

- **Authentic** (the default) shows only the model's own controls.
- **Ideal** shows every control, under its generic name where the model has none. A control the circuit does not have still does nothing.

Switch views on the amp page, or set the default in Preferences.

### Dialling the front panel

1. **Set the gain first.** Gain changes how the tone controls sound, so set it before the EQ.
2. **Start the tone stack at noon.** Then turn one knob through its whole range to hear what it does on this model. Passive tone stacks interact: on a Fender or a Marshall, the Bass and Treble change the mids too, and turning the Middle down also lowers the volume.
3. **Presence and Depth last.** They act on the power amp, not the preamp (see [Negative feedback, presence and depth](#negative-feedback-presence-and-depth)).
4. **Master, then Level.** On a master-volume amp, set **Master** for the amount of power-amp breakup you want, then set **Level** for loudness.

> **Note:** Changing the Type resets every control to the new model's defaults, except **Level**, **Balance** and **Input Select**. Note settings you want to keep, or keep two models on two channels of the block.

### Generic switches

Most models also have a few switches that are not on the real amp, for when the circuit lacks one:

- **Fat** lowers the tone stack's frequencies, for a thicker sound.
- **Input Cut** puts a 150 Hz high-pass filter at the input, to tighten the low end before the preamp.
- **Presence Shift** moves the presence an octave down.
- **High Treble** (5 is flat) is a shelf at 3 kHz before the power amp.
- **Overdrive** adds up to 20 dB more swing into the last gain stage.

## Master volume and power-amp breakup

A tube amp distorts in two places. The **preamp** distorts when its small tubes are driven hard: a fizzier, tighter, more defined distortion. The **power amp** distorts when the output tubes run out of swing: a thicker, more compressed, "blooming" distortion with a punchy low end, plus the effects of sag and the speaker load.

- On amps **without a master** (most Fenders, early Marshalls, Vox), the volume controls set both: turning up drives the preamp and the power amp together. That is why these models sound best "cranked".
- On amps **with a master**, the gain sets the preamp and the **Master** the power amp. Low Master: preamp distortion, tight and controlled. High Master: the power amp joins in.

Unlike a real amp, Cathode's loudness does not follow the Master: the amp's **Level** sets what you hear. So you can run the Master high at any volume. **Master Trim** (Power Amp page) changes how far the master turns, so it reaches full earlier or never.

## The expert pages

Behind the front panel, the expert pages hold the components of the circuit. Every one of them starts at the model's stock value (100 %, Stock or the middle of the range), so you can ignore them all and still get the real amp. Change them to explore "what if": a different tube, a softer supply, a hotter bias. The [Amp block reference](/manual/blocks/circuitamp) lists every control; this section explains the ideas.

### Power Amp

<!-- screenshot wanted: the Amp block on its Power Amp page (Sag, Bias, Feedback, Power Tube, Variac, Supply Type, Rectifier) -->

**Sag.** A tube amp's power supply cannot deliver unlimited current. When you hit a loud chord, the supply voltage drops for a moment and recovers, which compresses the attack and makes the note swell. **Sag** (5 is stock) scales the supply's internal resistance from a quarter (0, stiff and fast) to four times stock (10, soft and spongy). **B+ Time Const** on the Advanced page sets how quickly the supply recovers.

**Rectifier.** The rectifier turns the mains into DC. A tube rectifier (**GZ34**, **5U4**, **5Y3**, **EZ81**) drops more voltage the more current you draw, adding sag; **Silicon** is stiff. **Stock** is the model's own.

**Supply Type.** **DC** is an ideal supply; **AC 60 Hz** and **AC 50 Hz** model rectified mains with its ripple, which modulates the sound subtly at the mains frequency under heavy playing.

**Variac.** The mains voltage, 50 to 130 %. Lower voltage means less headroom, earlier and spongier breakup, and a darker sound: the trick of running an amp on a variac for a "brown" tone. The Factory "Brown Sound" preset runs its Plexi 100W at 85 %.

**Bias.** How much current the output tubes draw at rest. 50 % is stock; lower is colder, higher hotter. A cold bias gives more crossover distortion (a gritty, sputtering quality as notes decay), a hot one a smoother, warmer, earlier breakup. On a cathode-biased amp (many class A designs) the knob changes the cathode resistor; on a fixed-bias amp, the bias voltage.

**Feedback.** Negative feedback takes some of the output back to the phase inverter to flatten the response and tighten the low end. 5 is stock, 0 is none, 10 is twice stock. Less feedback sounds looser, rawer, louder and brighter in the upper mids, and breaks up earlier; more is tighter and cleaner. Some models switch feedback themselves (the Rectifiers' **Modern** removes it).

**Power Tube.** Replace the output tubes with **6L6GC**, **EL34**, **EL84**, **6V6GT**, **KT66**, **KT88** or **6550**, rebiased to the design's idle current (for a 5881, pick 6L6GC: electrically it is the same tube). Broadly: 6L6 and 5881 are clean and full with a firm low end (the American sound); EL34 is mid-forward and crunchy (the British sound); EL84 is chimey and compresses early; 6V6 is sweet and breaks up early; KT88 and 6550 have huge headroom and a tight, big low end; KT66 is warm and round.

**Presence Freq** and **Depth Freq** move the presence and depth controls' frequencies.

**Bias Trem Rate** and **Bias Trem Depth** add a bias tremolo (a tremolo made by swinging the output tubes' bias, for a throbbing, slightly distorted tremolo). On a model with its own tremolo or vibrato, these become its **Speed** and **Intensity**.

**Power Amp** off leaves the power amp out, so the output is the preamp alone: use it to feed a real tube power amp. **Plate Suppressor Diodes** and **Master Location** (Stock, or Post PI: the master after the phase inverter, as on some modified amps) are for the curious.

### Negative feedback, presence and depth

The **Presence** and **Depth** controls work inside the feedback loop: they make the feedback weaker at high frequencies (Presence) or low frequencies (Depth), so the power amp boosts those frequencies. That is why they sound different from a treble or bass knob: they shape the power amp's response and its breakup, not the preamp. With little feedback they have less to work with. Models name Depth their own way (**Resonance**, **Deep**, **Depth Punch**, **Depth Boost**).

The Vox-style **Cut** control is different: it cuts treble in the phase inverter, so more Cut means a darker sound.

### Preamp

**Input Trim** changes the level at the amp's input without changing its tone, like a hotter or weaker pickup. **Input Select** chooses which input side the amp takes (it processes mono). **Low Cut** is a gentle high-pass before the first stage: the classic way to tighten a high-gain amp without thinning the sound after it.

**Preamp Tube** replaces every preamp tube (12AX7 is the usual one) with a **12AY7**, **5751**, **12AT7** or **12AU7**. These have less gain than a 12AX7 (the 5751 a little less, the 12AU7 far less), for more headroom and a cleaner, more open sound. **Preamp Sag** sets how much of the supply's sag reaches the preamp.

**Boost Type** (Off, TS808 OD, Super OD, Chiron OD, Treble Booster, Esoteric Boost, FET Boost) puts a drive pedal in front of the input, with **Boost Drive**, **Boost Tone** and **Boost Level**, without a separate block. **Saturation** adds a diode-style soft clipper between the preamp and the power amp. **Definition** tilts the input towards treble (positive) or bass (negative).

**Tonestack Type** swaps in another model's tone stack, and **Tonestack Location** moves it earlier or later in the preamp. A stack early in the chain (as on Fenders) changes what gets distorted; a stack late in the chain (as on many high-gain amps) shapes the distortion after it happens.

### Advanced

The Advanced page scales component values the model already has, and hides any the model lacks.

- **Bright Cap**: the bright capacitors across the volume pots, which keep the treble when the volume is low.
- **Preamp Bias**: the last gain stage's operating point, and so its even harmonics.
- **Stack Freq**: the tone stack's frequencies.
- **Xfmr Match**, **Xfmr Low Freq**, **Xfmr High Freq**, **Xfmr Drive**: the output transformer. Its impedance ratio, its bandwidth, and core saturation, which makes low notes at full power compress and thicken.
- **Cathode Time Const**: a cathode-biased power amp's bypass capacitor, which sets how the bias shifts under hard playing.
- **Power Tube Hardness** and **Preamp Hardness**: how sharply the tubes clip (5 is stock).
- **Bias Excursion** controls (preamp, phase inverter, power tubes, and a master over all three) with **Excursion Time** and **Recovery Time**: the grid current that shifts the bias when a tube is driven hard. This is what gives an overdriven amp its "blocking" (a brief choke after a big hit) and its bloom.
- **CF Compression** and **CF Harmonics**: a cathode follower's behaviour, on models that have one.
- **Screen Freq** and **Screen Q**: the power tubes' screen supply.

### Speaker

A tube power amp has a fairly high output impedance, so its response follows the speaker's impedance: more output at the speaker's low resonance (thump) and at the top, where the voice coil's inductance raises the impedance. Less feedback makes this stronger. This happens inside the amp; the sound of the cone and the mic are the Cab block's.

- **Speaker load**: **Stock** is the model's own load; then curves for **1x12 Open**, **2x12 Open**, **4x10 Open**, **4x12 Closed** and **8x10 Closed**; and **Follow Cab**, which takes the load of the speaker and cabinet in the next Cab block, from that speaker's own data. Use Follow Cab: the amp and cab then agree. The Factory presets do.
- **Load resonance**, **Load Q**, **Load peak**, **Load HF** and **Load impedance** scale that curve: the resonance frequency, its sharpness, its height, the top-end rise and the overall impedance (50 % is like a 4 Ω cab on an 8 Ω output).
- **Find the cab resonance…**, below the amp's controls, plays a test tone through the cab while you sweep it to where the low end booms loudest, then sets **Load resonance** to match.
- **Speaker Drive**, **Speaker Thump** (at **Cab Resonance**) and **Speaker Compression** add the speaker's own breakup, low-end thump and compression.
- **Output Mode**: **FRFR** for monitors or a flat speaker; **SS Power Amp + Cab** when Cathode drives a real guitar cabinet through a solid-state power amp, which leaves the speaker stages to the real cone.

### EQ

**Input Filter** (shelves, a peak, a high-pass or low-pass, with frequency, Q and gain) shapes the signal into the preamp. **Output EQ** adds a graphic EQ (from 3 to 10 bands) at the **Output EQ Location**: the output, before the power amp, or the input. EQ before the power amp shapes what the power amp distorts; EQ at the output only shapes the result.

### Dynamics

**Input Dynamics** expands (positive) or compresses (negative) the input by its envelope: positive values give back some of the touch sensitivity high gain takes away. **Output Comp** (Off, Output, Feedback) is a compressor after the amp, with **Amount**, **Threshold** and **Clarity**.
